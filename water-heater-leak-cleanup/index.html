Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Pembroke Pines: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

Our Pembroke Pines team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • How long the tank kept refilling before the supply valve was closed.
  • Any rebuilding of the subfloor or platform beneath the tank ahead of the new heater.
  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.
  • The amount of wall drywall, ceiling drywall or insulation that has to be removed.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Heater Closet Drywall

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Adjacent Room Flooring

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

What We Do

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
