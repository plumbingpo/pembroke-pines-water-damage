Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at Pembroke Pines Restoration Co are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage

Structural Drying, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

Hygrometers, moisture meters and a thermal camera let us map moisture across each area that was affected. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Setting the Equipment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Monitoring

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.
