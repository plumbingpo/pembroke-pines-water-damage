Kitchen Water Damage in Pembroke Pines

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. Our Pembroke Pines crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

The Dishwasher Cavity

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

What We Do

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.
