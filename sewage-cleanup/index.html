Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
